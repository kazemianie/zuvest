At $126.16 the stock trades at 45.7x trailing and 39.1x forward earnings, with a PEG of 4.33 and a free-cash-flow yield of 1.49%. That is a lot to pay for roughly 5% growth. The Fundamental analyst's buy zone is $85–100, well below today's price.

Long-term investment view

Stance: neutral / wait. Quality grade A, valuation unattractive.

Using the mosaic framework, the long-term horizon should lean mainly on fundamentals, valuation, forensic and macro evidence. Here is how each lens reads:

  • Is it a great business? Yes.
  • Revenue is $713B, with a 5.3% growth rate and +5.94% in the latest quarter.
  • ROIC of 15.7% is well above the WACC of 8.27%, which points to a scale/cost moat.
  • Net debt/EBITDA is 1.21x and interest coverage is 11.5x, so the balance sheet is sound.
  • Forensic verdict: CLEAN. Beneish M is −2.55 (below the −1.78 warning line), Altman Z is 6.66 and accruals are 5.0%.
  • Is the price attractive? No.
  • Forward P/E of 39.1x and a PEG of 4.33 are expensive. A PEG above 2 is usually considered pricey.
  • The FCF yield of 1.49% is below the 10-year Treasury yield of 5.06%. You earn less cash yield from the stock than from a "risk-free" bond, so you are betting heavily on future growth.
  • The reverse DCF (working backward from the price to the growth it implies) shows ~38% implied starting growth on current free cash flow. Historical growth is 5.3%.
  • The Fundamental analyst rejected its own mechanical DCF ($21.56 / $30.52 / $37.01) as too punitive, because trailing FCF is depressed. It used a multiples blend instead: bear $60 / base $85 / bull $110.
  • Teaching illustration only: applying 25/50/25 weights gives 0.25×110 + 0.5×85 + 0.25×60 = $85. That is about 33% below the current price, so there is no margin of safety.
  • What is priced in?
  • The analyst mean target of $131.9 implies only +4.55% upside, despite a "strong buy" consensus from 41 analysts.
  • The stock has fallen after most earnings reports despite beating EPS estimates in 7 of 8 quarters. In other words, good news is usually already in the price.
  • Watch item: FCF conversion fell from 103% to 68%. The cause (capex vs working capital) is unknown.

Thesis framing: "The market prices Walmart as a growth stock (~39x forward) for its e-commerce, advertising and membership pivot. I see an excellent ~5% grower whose good news is largely priced in."

What would prove this cautious view wrong: - Sustained operating-margin expansion (currently 4.2%) from advertising and membership. - EPS growth well above 10%. - FCF conversion recovering to 100% or more. - A materially lower interest-rate environment.

Thesis breakers if you already own it: - Operating margin below ~3.5%. - Revenue growth below ~3%. - ROIC sliding toward ~8%.

Walmart is a high-quality, defensive retailer with steady ~5% growth, a 15.7% ROIC and low leverage. At $126.16 it trades at 45.7x trailing and 39.1x forward earnings with a 1.49% FCF yield, which is expensive for a ~5% grower. The mechanical DCF ($30) is too punitive, so I raise the fair value to a multiples-based range, but even that sits below the price. Good business, but the price already assumes a lot.

Key findings

  • Steady growth and a defensive franchise Revenue CAGR 5.3%, latest quarter +5.94% yoy, revenue $713B
  • Margins are thin but stable to slightly improving Gross margin 24.9%, operating margin 4.2% (4.3% prior year, 3.3% two years earlier), net margin 3.1%
  • ROIC is above WACC, which indicates a moat (scale and cost advantage) ROIC 15.7% vs WACC 8.27%; ROE 22%
  • Cash conversion is weaker than before and FCF is small relative to the price FCF $14.9B, conversion 68% (vs 103% two years earlier), FCF yield 1.49% vs 10-year yield 5.06%
  • Balance sheet is sound Net debt/EBITDA 1.21x, interest coverage 11.5x, current ratio 0.79 (normal for a retailer), goodwill 10.1% of assets
  • Valuation is very rich relative to growth P/E 45.7x, forward P/E 39.1x, PEG 4.33 (>2 is expensive), EV/EBITDA 23.3x

Risks

  • Multiple compression: a premium of ~39x forward earnings leaves no margin of safety if growth or sentiment disappoints.
  • Earnings are reported today (EPS estimate 0.64, revenue estimate $187.25B), so there is event risk.
  • Thin operating margin (4.2%) means small cost or pricing pressure moves profit a lot.
  • Falling FCF conversion (68%) may reflect heavy capex or working capital.
  • A 10-year yield of 5.06% pressures long-duration equities.
