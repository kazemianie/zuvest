Long-term investment view

Stance: neutral, wait for a better price or better evidence. The fundamental analyst rates the business quality grade A but sets the stance at neutral, with 55% confidence.

What is great about the business: - Operating margin is 60.4%. - FCF (free cash flow) is $96.7B, with 81% conversion. Why the price is still not attractive: - The valuation tools conflict. Cheap multiples (forward P/E 9.2, PEG 0.19) say "bargain." The mechanical DCF gives a probability-weighted value of about $91. The reverse DCF says today's price already assumes about 40% starting FCF growth, against a 25% base case. - A reverse DCF works backward from the current price to find the growth the market is already assuming. - When valuation tools disagree this much says to assume the market may know something you don't. - The DCF may be too harsh. It uses a 13% WACC built on a beta of 1.6. The analyst adjusted the values up to bear $54, base $105 and bull $165. Even the adjusted base case is below $152. - Growth is slowing from a very high level. Revenue growth went 125.9% → 114.2% → 65.5%. Gross margin slipped from 75.0% to 71.1%. - Forensic verdict: WATCH, not red flag. - The Beneish M-Score is -1.27, above the -1.78 threshold, but it is mostly driven by fast growth. - The cash-flow accruals ratio is 59.4%. - Inventory grew 112% against revenue growth of 65%. If demand cools, write-downs could follow. - Data problems lower confidence. The trailing P/E (19.2) and forward P/E (9.2) do not reconcile. The earnings release on 2026-11-17 is out, but its figures and guidance are unknown in our data.

What would prove the wait-stance wrong (falsification): - Turn bullish if: the actual results and guidance show growth well above 40% with gross margin stabilizing at about 70% or higher. - Turn bearish if: hyperscalers cut capex, gross margin falls below about 65%, or revenue growth drops under 20%. - Consider buying if: the price reaches the $100–120 zone, which would offer a real margin of safety.
