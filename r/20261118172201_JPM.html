Long-term investment view

The business is excellent, but the price is not a bargain. ROE (return on equity, meaning profit per dollar of shareholder capital) is 15.7%, against a cost of equity of about 10.1%. So JPM creates value. Revenue has compounded at 12.5% a year, and the share count fell 6.35% through buybacks. The payout ratio is a modest 26% (/).

Why "hold" rather than "buy": banks are valued on price-to-book (P/B, the price relative to the accounting value of equity), not on a DCF. The negative FCF of −$147.8B reflects balance-sheet flows and is meaningless for a bank. Using ROE of 15.7% and growth of 4–5%, the justified P/B comes out at about 1.9–2.1x. The stock trades at 2.47x, so the market is already paying a premium.

Illustrative scenario math. The fundamental analyst's values are bear $250, base $315 and bull $375. With assumed weights of 25/50/25, the expected value is about $314, roughly 4% below today's price. These weights are my illustration, not analyst output. The result shows there is no margin of safety. Analysts are more optimistic, with a mean target of $373.48 (+14.2%). Their targets span from Citi's Neutral at $350 to Barclays' Overweight at $425.

There is no variant perception here. teaches that being right about a great company is not enough; you must differ from the market and explain why it is wrong. Our view (high-quality, fairly priced) is essentially the consensus view. So the edge is a patience edge: wait for the price to come to you.

Forensics (accounting checks). No red flags appear on the available signals, with a CLEAN verdict at modest confidence. The standard manipulation scores (Beneish, Altman, Piotroski) do not apply to banks. Two items still need reading: - An 8-K Item 5.02 officer change filed 17 Nov. Who left is unknown. - A batch of 424B2 filings on 18 Nov. These are probably structured notes, but that is unverified.

Thesis breakers (written in advance): - Charge-offs and provisions rising while reserve coverage falls. - ROE sliding toward the ~10% cost of equity. - CET1 capital nearing the regulatory minimum, or a cut to capital returns. - Revenue turning negative as net interest income falls.

The biggest data gap: NIM, CET1, ROTCE, NCO ratio and reserve coverage are all unknown. Treat every long-term conclusion above as provisional until you have them.
